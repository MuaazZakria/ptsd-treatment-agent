Use of medicinal cannabis in the treatment of insomnia in adults

Authors: Danielle Rosado Giopato, Maria Oliveira, Larissa Cruz Gatti, Erika Duarte Pedro, C. Santos
Journal: São Paulo Medical Journal
Date: 2025
DOI: 10.5327/1516-3180.cpn.2001
Citations: 0
URL: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5403b01d76258e34f7344d4608a798c64188a402

ABSTRACT: Introduction: Insomnia is the most prevalent sleep-wake disorder worldwide, affecting approximately 10% of the global population. In addition to its direct impact on the physiological sleep process—such as impairing the body’s functional restoration and memory consolidation—insomnia also affects patients’ quality of life, predisposing them to interpersonal issues and the development of other medical conditions, including psychiatric disorders. Currently, cannabinoid compounds such as Δ-9-tetrahydrocannabinol and cannabidiol, derived from the Cannabis sativa plant, are being studied as potential treatments for this sleep disorder. By acting on the endocannabinoid system, these compounds produce a dose-dependent sedative effect by interacting with specific receptors. Thus, cannabinoids have emerged as promising targets for research in the pursuit of safe and effective therapies for insomnia.
Objective: The aim of this study was to analyze the efficacy and safety of medicinal cannabis in the treatment of insomnia in adults.
Methods: The study included individuals diagnosed with insomnia who had some experience using medicinal cannabis as a treatment. The analyzed variables were the effects of cannabis on sleep quality and duration, sleep latency, insomnia-related symptoms, side effects, tolerance, and patients’ opinions on the treatment. The research was conducted in the BVS, PubMed, and Cochrane databases. Articles published more than 10 years ago and those without full-text availability were excluded. The selection was also limited to studies in English, Portuguese, and Spanish. Exclusion criteria were applied based on title screening, abstract review, and full-text analysis. A total of 248 articles were identified, and after applying these criteria, 11 studies were included in the final database.
Results: The therapeutic effect of cannabis showed promise in improving both primary insomnia and secondary insomnia associated with medical conditions, particularly those related to pain, such as fibromyalgia. Clinical trial data indicated that certain cannabinoids not only reduce sleep latency and nighttime awakenings but also increase total sleep duration and patients’ subjective sleep quality. The effect of cannabinoids on insomnia appears to be dose-dependent and influenced by different combinations and types of cannabinoids used. Additionally, continuous cannabis use may lead to tolerance to some of its beneficial effects on sleep. Finally, depending on the dose, side effects were reported, with the most common being dry mouth and nausea.
Conclusion: Although many of the analyzed data come from studies with small sample sizes, short treatment periods, and certain biases in clinical trial evaluations, the results of this review suggest that medicinal cannabis is a promising therapeutic option for treating insomnia in adults and, therefore, warrants further investigation.
